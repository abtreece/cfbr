Betting guide

What the numbers on the betting pages mean, and the rules of thumb behind using them. The games in the examples are made up. Nothing here is betting advice.

Reading a spread

A spread handicaps the favorite so either side is a fair bet. In Riverton −7 vs Lakeview +7, Riverton is favored by 7:

  • A bet on Riverton −7 wins if Riverton wins by 8 or more. That's covering.
  • A bet on Lakeview +7 wins if Lakeview wins, or loses by 6 or less.
  • If Riverton wins by exactly 7, it's a push: the bet is refunded.
  • A half point (−7.5) can't push. Riverton must win by 8; a 7-point win now loses.

The betting pages always write a line from the side being bet, so Lakeview +7 and Riverton −7 are the same game seen from opposite sides.

The price (juice)

The number in parentheses, like (−110), is the price in American odds. A minus price is what you risk to win $100; a plus price is what you win on $100. With both sides at −110, a bettor on each side risks $110 to win $100; that extra $10 is the book's cut, called juice or vig. More juice is a worse payout: you risk more to win the same amount, so you need to win more often to break even.

Consensus and your book

  • Consensus is the median spread across the books in the regular line feeds, offshore books included. It's the market's best guess, and the number paper bets are recorded and graded at. Lines fetched only to price a bet{% if "bet365" in rules.pricing_books %} (bet365's){% endif %} are marked "pricing only" on the bets pages and never count toward it.
  • Your book's number is what you can actually bet. Books differ by a half point or a point, and by price.
  • On the shortlist, "−3.5 or better at DraftKings" means DraftKings had −3.5 or a friendlier number (−3, −2.5) when the bet was recorded. For an underdog, "or better" means a bigger number (+7.5 beats +7).
  • Offshore books (Bovada, BetOnline and others) count toward the consensus as market information, but a paper bet is never priced at one.

Line moves

Lines move when money comes in on one side or news breaks (an injury, a suspension, the weather). Under each shortlist pick is what its book offers in the newest odds snapshot:

  • Better: a friendlier number than at capture (−3 instead of −3.5). Good news if you haven't bet yet.
  • Worse: you'd need more points (−4.5 instead of −3.5). If you haven't bet, the bet got more expensive. If you already bet the old number, the market has moved your way: you're ahead of it (see closing line value).
  • Edge now is the model's margin against the number now. Under {{ "%g"|format(rules.threshold) }} points, the model wouldn't bet it.

Snapshots are taken a few times a day, so a live book can be ahead of the page. Check your book before you bet.

Key numbers

Football is scored in 3s and 7s, so some final margins are far more common than others. In FBS-vs-FBS games from 2015 to 2025, 3 was the final margin in about 10% of games and 7 in about 9%; 10 and 14 each about 4.5%. Most other margins are 3.5% or less.

A half point that crosses a key number changes the result in many more games than one that doesn't. Moving from −2.5 to −3.5 turns every 3-point win from a win into a loss; moving from −11.5 to −12.5 only changes 12-point wins. When a pick says "crossed 7", the move went across one of these numbers and cost (or gained) more than its size suggests.

Points vs price

Books trade points for price. For one game you might see −13.5 (−115) at one book and −14.5 (−105) at another. The first costs more juice; the second needs one more point, and 14 is a key number. Here the extra point is usually worth more than the 10 cents of juice. Away from a key number (−11.5 vs −12.5), the cheaper price usually wins.

The shortlist weighs both: its "or better at" book is the one with the best expected value given the number and the price, not just the best number.

Edge, and what it isn't

Edge is how far the model's margin is from the line. If the model has Riverton by 10 and the line is Riverton −7, the edge is 3 points on Riverton. A bet is made when the edge is at least {{ "%g"|format(rules.threshold) }} points.

The edge decides whether to bet. It isn't a measure of how sure the model is. Bigger edges haven't covered more often:

The edge is the model's error minus the market's error. The market's error is usually small, because it already prices in everything public. So a large edge more often means the model is missing something, like a starting quarterback's injury, than that the market is badly wrong. {{ MODEL }} rates teams from play-by-play efficiency and doesn't see injury reports.

Closing line value

The closing line is the last line before kickoff, when the market has the most information. Closing line value (CLV) is how much better your number was than the close: bet Riverton −7, game closes −8.5, you have +1.5 points of CLV.

Over many bets, beating the close is the best sign of a real edge. A season's win–loss record is mostly luck: over 100 bets, a bettor with no edge at all wins 60 or more about 3% of the time and 55 or more about 18% of the time. That's why the paper bets draw no conclusion before {{ rules.min_sample }} settled bets, and why average CLV is shown first.

Rules of thumb

  1. Same number at two books: take the less negative price. −105 beats −110.
  2. Same price: take the better number.
  3. Number and price both differ: a half point across 3 or 7 is worth more than a few cents of juice. Elsewhere, the price usually wins.
  4. Check your book right before you bet. The site's lines can be hours old.
  5. If the line moved past the "or better" number, look at "edge now". Under {{ "%g"|format(rules.threshold) }}, pass.
  6. A big line move or a big edge is a reason to check the news (injuries, suspensions, weather) before betting.
  7. A bigger edge isn't a surer bet. Don't bet more because the edge is larger.
  8. Bet a flat amount. Every paper bet is {{ "%g"|format(rules.stake) }} {{ "unit" if rules.stake == 1 else "units" }} (${{ "%g"|format(unit_risk) }} to win ${{ "%g"|format(unit_win) }}), where a unit is ${{ "%g"|format(rules.unit_value) }}, {{ (rules.unit_value / rules.bankroll)|pct(1) }} of a ${{ "{:,.0f}".format(rules.bankroll) }} bankroll. Never chase losses.
  9. Judge yourself on CLV, not on a week's record.
  10. At {{ rules.odds|price }} you need {{ break_even_std|pct }} to break even. Most bettors don't get there, and none of the model's holdout tiers above did.

Glossary

ATS
Against the spread: a team's or a model's record at covering, rather than winning outright.
Bankroll
The money set aside for betting. Bet sizes are a fixed share of it.
Closing line
The last line before kickoff.
CLV
Closing line value: how many points better your number was than the closing line.
Consensus
The median line across books.
Cover
Win the bet against the spread.
Edge
Points between the model's margin and the line.
Favorite / underdog
The team giving points (−) / getting points (+).
Juice, vig
The book's cut, built into the price.
Key number
A common final margin (3, 7, 10, 14) where a half point matters most.
Line, spread
The points the favorite gives the underdog.
Paper bet
A bet the tracker records and grades but never places.
Price, odds
What a bet pays, in American odds: −110 risks $110 to win $100.
Push
A result exactly on the line; the bet is refunded.
Sharp
A bettor (or book) the market treats as well informed; sharp money moves lines.
Shortlist
The paper bets that one of your books offered at the recorded number or better.
